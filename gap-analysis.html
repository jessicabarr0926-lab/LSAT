7Sage and LSAT Demon win because they reduce decision fatigue, force review discipline, and turn every section into measurable feedback. JessiPreps should prioritize timed sections, adaptive drills, Blind Review, family mastery, score trend, and history before community, admissions, and live-class systems.

Priority order

Top 12 build list

1. Timed Section mode

Cause: practice stays fragmented. Effect: pacing stamina and section analytics stay weak.

2. Adaptive Drill button + engine

Cause: manual drill choice creates decision fatigue. Effect: weak areas get avoided.

3. Forced Blind Review workflow

Cause: explanations unlock too early. Effect: misses become consumption instead of diagnosis.

4. Per-family mastery rating

Cause: one score hides question-family movement. Effect: the student cannot see what is driving gains.

5. Score-trajectory chart

Cause: snapshots are motivationally thin. Effect: progress feels invisible between tests.

6. Persistent left rail with sub-items

Cause: core loops need to be one click away. Effect: resume and review paths become easier to follow.

7. Streak counter

Cause: daily return has no product reward. Effect: consistency relies too much on willpower.

8. Study plan with test date

Cause: no deadline means no urgency model. Effect: recommendations cannot adapt to remaining weeks.

9. Journal editor

Cause: wrong answers need structure. Effect: misses turn into reusable rules instead of loose notes.

10. Practice Test mode

Cause: section skill must transfer to full-test endurance. Effect: students are underprepared for 4-section rhythm.

11. Section / Drill history

Cause: completed work needs a record. Effect: resume, filters, notes, and trend analysis become possible.

12. Explanations library

Cause: review content is fragmented. Effect: students cannot search patterns across questions.

Gap categories

What exists, what is missing, what it causes.

Core mechanics

Needs adaptive sessions, timed sections, full PT mode, skip-protected blocks, resume state, embedded You Try exercises, and bookmarks.

Review loop

Needs forced Blind Review, section review tools, due-today SRS, variance, and a searchable explanation index.

Analytics

Needs overview, priorities, question analytics, score trends, time buckets, official-test tracking, and study-quality scoring.

Planning

Needs test date, law-school cycle awareness, weekly workload planning, and skip-block confirmations.

UX tooling

Needs streaks, date filters, notifications, notes templates, onboarding, KPI explainers, and privacy controls.

Content strategy

Cannot copy official PrepTests without licensing, so the site should compete on original practice, explanations, review quality, and official-link workflows.

Community

Forums, live sessions, office hours, profiles, chat, and leaderboards are valuable later but depend on audience and moderation.

Admissions

Application tracking, school data, decision tracking, and scholarship tools are downstream features to defer until LSAT retention is proven.

Pricing

Subscribe CTAs and premium gates should wait until the core loop is clearly valuable enough to monetize.

Polish

Tighten visual hierarchy, reduce soft-lavender dominance, add row hover metadata, and add feedback widgets.
